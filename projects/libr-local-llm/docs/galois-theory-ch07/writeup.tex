\begin{problem}{13(a)}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $\zeta=\zeta_p$, $K=\QQ(\zeta)$ and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$. Show that $G$ is cyclic of order $p-1$.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION 13(a) =====
Each $\sigma\in G$ sends $\zeta$ to a root of $\Phi_p$, so $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^k$ for some $k\in\{1,\dots,p-1\}$. Conversely, for every such $k$ there is exactly one automorphism with $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^k$, because the roots of the minimal polynomial are exactly the primitive $p$-th roots. Hence $|G|=p-1$.

The map $\sigma\mapsto k$ is injective, since $\sigma$ is determined by $\sigma(\zeta)$, and it turns composition into multiplication: $\sigma_j\circ\sigma_k=\sigma_{jk}$, with exponents read mod $p$. So $G\cong(\ZZ/p\ZZ)^\times$.

The group $(\ZZ/p\ZZ)^\times$ is cyclic, by the named result that the multiplicative group of a finite field is cyclic. Therefore $G$ is cyclic of order $p-1$.
% ===== END SOLUTION 13(a) =====

\begin{problem}{13(b)}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $\zeta_p$ a primitive $p$-th root of unity, $K=\QQ(\zeta_p)$, and $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$, cyclic of order $p-1$. For each divisor $d$ of $p-1$, show that there is exactly one intermediate field $K_d$ with $[K_d:\QQ]=d$, and that $K_d$ is Galois over $\QQ$ with cyclic Galois group.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION 13(b) =====
Fix a divisor $d$ of $p-1$. Since $G$ is cyclic of order $p-1$, it has exactly one subgroup of order $(p-1)/d$; call it $H$. By the correspondence, $H$ has a fixed field $K^H$, and
\[
[K^H:\QQ]=[G:H]=\frac{|G|}{|H|}=\frac{p-1}{(p-1)/d}=d.
\]
So a field of degree $d$ exists.

For uniqueness, let $L$ be any intermediate field with $[L:\QQ]=d$. By the correspondence $L=K^{H'}$ for a unique subgroup $H'$, and $[K^{H'}:\QQ]=[G:H']$ gives $|H'|=(p-1)/d$. A cyclic group has only one subgroup of each order, so $H'=H$ and $L=K^H$.

Finally, every subgroup of a cyclic group is normal, so $H\unlhd G$. Therefore $K^H/\QQ$ is Galois with group $G/H$. A quotient of a cyclic group is cyclic, so $\operatorname{Gal}(K_d/\QQ)$ is cyclic.
% ===== END SOLUTION 13(b) =====

\begin{problem}{13(d)}
Let $p$ be an odd prime, $\zeta=\zeta_p$, $K=\QQ(\zeta)$, $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$, and $H\le G$ a subgroup of index $d$, read as a set of residues in $(\ZZ/p\ZZ)^\times$. Let $\eta_H=\sum_{a\in H}\zeta^a$ be the Gaussian period. Show that $K_d=K^H=\QQ(\eta_H)$.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION 13(d) =====
\textbf{The case $p=13$, $d=2$.} Let $H=\{1,3,4,9,10,12\}$ be the nonzero squares mod $13$ and $N=\{2,5,6,7,8,11\}$ the nonsquares, with $\eta_H=\sum_{a\in H}\zeta^a$ and $\eta_N=\sum_{b\in N}\zeta^b$.

Since $3\in H$ and $H$ is a subgroup, multiplication by $3$ permutes $H$, so $\sigma_3(\eta_H)=\eta_H$. Since $2\notin H$, $\sigma_2(\eta_H)=\eta_N$: the exponents $2,6,8,18,20,24$ reduce to $2,6,8,5,7,11$, which is $N$. And $\sigma_2^2=\sigma_4$ with $4\in H$, so $\sigma_2^2(\eta_H)=\eta_H$. The stabilizer of $\eta_H$ is therefore exactly $H$, and its orbit is $\{\eta_H,\eta_N\}$.

The two periods are distinct. If $\eta_H=\eta_N$, the difference has coefficient $+1$ on $\zeta^a$ for $a\in H$ and $-1$ for $a\in N$; those twelve coefficients are rational and not all zero, so the difference cannot vanish, because $\zeta,\dots,\zeta^{12}$ is a $\QQ$-basis of $K$.

The orbit has size $[G:H]=2$, so $[\QQ(\eta_H):\QQ]=2$. Since $\eta_H$ is fixed by $H$, $\QQ(\eta_H)\subseteq K^H$, and both have degree $2$ over $\QQ$; hence $\QQ(\eta_H)=K^H=K_2$. This proves the claim for $p=13$, $d=2$.

The concrete case also gives $\eta_H+\eta_N=-1$, since $H\cup N=\{1,\dots,12\}$ and $\sum_{i=1}^{12}\zeta^i=-1$, and $3N=N$ because $3\in H$. For the product, $m_0=0$ and $m_1=3$; the map $(a,b)\mapsto(2b,2a)$ sends $S_t$ one-to-one onto $S_{2t}$ with inverse $(c,d)\mapsto(7d,7c)$, so $m_{2t}=m_t$. Since $2$ has order $12$ mod $13$, every nonzero $t$ has $m_t=3$, and the $36$ pairs are accounted for. Hence $\eta_H\eta_N=3\sum_{i=1}^{12}\zeta^i=-3$, and $\eta_H,\eta_N$ are the roots of $x^2+x-3$.

\textbf{The general case: the periods are pairwise distinct.} Suppose $\eta_{aH}=\eta_{bH}$ for distinct cosets $aH\ne bH$, and let $P(x)=\sum_{h\in H}x^{ah}-\sum_{h\in H}x^{bh}$. Then $P(\zeta)=0$, and the coefficients of $P$ are $+1$ on the exponents in $aH$, $-1$ on the exponents in $bH$, and $0$ elsewhere.

The polynomial $\Phi_p$ is the minimal polynomial of $\zeta$ over $\QQ$, so $\Phi_p$ divides $P$. Both have degree at most $p-1$, so $P=c\,\Phi_p$ for some constant $c$.

Since $aH$ and $bH$ are disjoint and nonempty, $P$ has a $+1$ coefficient and a $-1$ coefficient. Hence $P\ne0$, so $c\ne0$. But every coefficient of $c\,\Phi_p$ equals $c$, so no constant $c$ can match a coefficient list containing both $+1$ and $-1$. Contradiction. Therefore distinct cosets give distinct periods.

\textbf{The general case: the conjugation step.} Put $m(x)=\prod_{aH\in G/H}(x-\eta_{aH})$, a monic polynomial in $\QQ[x]$ of degree $d$ with $m(\eta_H)=0$. Suppose $m=fg$ with $f,g\in\QQ[x]$ monic of positive degree, and let $\alpha$ be a period that is a root of $f$. Every period is $\sigma(\alpha)$ for some $\sigma\in G$. Since $\sigma$ is a field automorphism fixing $\QQ$ pointwise, it is a ring homomorphism, so it commutes with evaluation of a rational-coefficient polynomial: $f(\sigma(\alpha))=\sigma(f(\alpha))=\sigma(0)=0$. Hence every period is a root of $f$.

\textbf{The remaining step.} The conjugation step gives $\deg f\ge d$, and $f\mid m$ gives $\deg f\le d$, so $f=m$ and $g$ is constant; that yields $[\QQ(\eta_H):\QQ]=d$ and $\QQ(\eta_H)=K_d$. What is still owed is the coefficient check behind $m\in\QQ[x]$: each coefficient of $m$ is symmetric in the periods, every $\sigma\in G$ permutes the periods, and $K^G=\QQ$. That check has not yet been written, so the argument above is not yet closed.
% ===== END SOLUTION 13(d) =====

\begin{problem}{problem-14a}
Problem 14(a). Show that $3$ is a primitive root modulo $7$, and deduce that $\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta_7)/\QQ)=\langle\sigma\rangle$ where $\sigma(\zeta_7)=\zeta_7^{3}$.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION problem-14a =====
The cyclic subgroup generated by each residue mod $7$ is
\[
\langle 2\rangle=\{2,4,1\},\qquad \langle 3\rangle=\{3,2,6,4,5,1\},\qquad \langle 5\rangle=\{5,4,6,2,3,1\}.
\]
So $2$ has order $3$ and is not a primitive root, while $3$ and $5$ have order $6$.

Take $\sigma(\zeta)=\zeta^{3}$. Since $3$ has order $6$, the powers $\sigma,\sigma^{2},\dots,\sigma^{6}$ send $\zeta$ to $\zeta^{3},\zeta^{2},\zeta^{6},\zeta^{4},\zeta^{5},\zeta^{1}$ respectively, which is exactly the six elements $\sigma_{3},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{6},\sigma_{4},\sigma_{5},\sigma_{1}$ of $\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$. Hence $\langle\sigma\rangle=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)$.
% ===== END SOLUTION problem-14a =====

\begin{problem}{problem-14b}
Problem 14(b). With $\alpha=\zeta+\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{4}$ and $\beta=\zeta^{3}+\zeta^{5}+\zeta^{6}$, show that $\alpha+\beta=-1$ and $\alpha\beta=2$, and deduce that the unique quadratic subfield of $K$ is $\QQ(\sqrt{-7})$.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION problem-14b =====
Between them, $\alpha$ and $\beta$ list each of $\zeta,\zeta^{2},\dots,\zeta^{6}$ exactly once, so
\[
\alpha+\beta=\zeta+\zeta^{2}+\cdots+\zeta^{6}=-1,
\]
because $\zeta$ is a root of $\Phi_7=x^{6}+x^{5}+\cdots+x+1$ and $\zeta\neq1$.

Expanding $\alpha\beta$ gives nine products, with exponents reduced modulo $7$:
\[
\zeta^{4},\ \zeta^{6},\ 1,\ \zeta^{5},\ 1,\ \zeta,\ 1,\ \zeta^{2},\ \zeta^{3}.
\]
Three of the nine are $\zeta^{7}=1$, and the other six are exactly $\alpha+\beta$. Hence
\[
\alpha\beta=3+(\alpha+\beta)=3-1=2.
\]

Since $\alpha+\beta=-1$ and $\alpha\beta=2$, the monic quadratic with roots $\alpha$ and $\beta$ is
\[
(x-\alpha)(x-\beta)=x^{2}-(\alpha+\beta)x+\alpha\beta=x^{2}+x+2.
\]
The quadratic formula with $a=1$, $b=1$, $c=2$ gives
\[
x=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{1-8}}{2}=\frac{-1\pm\sqrt{-7}}{2},
\]
so the two roots are $\frac{-1+\sqrt{-7}}{2}$ and $\frac{-1-\sqrt{-7}}{2}$. Either may be called $\alpha$; taking $\alpha$ to be the root with positive imaginary part matches the page's observation that the sines in $\alpha=\zeta+\zeta^{2}+\zeta^{4}$ have positive sum. Since $\sqrt{-7}\notin\QQ$, the polynomial is irreducible and $\QQ(\alpha)=\QQ(\sqrt{-7})$ has degree $2$ over $\QQ$.

Finally, $\QQ(\sqrt{-7})$ is the only subfield of $K$ of degree $2$ over $\QQ$. By 14(a), $G=\operatorname{Gal}(K/\QQ)=\langle\sigma\rangle$ is cyclic of order $6$, and a cyclic group has at most one subgroup of a given order. So $G$ has exactly one subgroup of order $3$, namely $\{e,\sigma^{2},\sigma^{4}\}$: the two generators $\sigma^{2}$ and $\sigma^{4}$ produce the same three elements. A subfield $L$ with $\QQ\subseteq L\subseteq K$ and $[L:\QQ]=2$ corresponds by the Galois correspondence to a subgroup $H\le G$ with $[G:H]=2$, that is $|H|=3$. There is only one such $H$, so there is only one such field, and $\QQ(\sqrt{-7})$ is it.
% ===== END SOLUTION problem-14b =====

\begin{problem}{problem-14c}
Problem 14(c). With $\zeta=\zeta_7$ and $\eta=\zeta+\zeta^{-1}=2\cos(2\pi/7)$, show that the minimal polynomial of $\eta$ over $\QQ$ is $x^3+x^2-2x-1$, and that $\QQ(\eta)$ is the unique cubic subfield of $K=\QQ(\zeta)$.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION problem-14c =====
The six values $\sigma^k(\eta)$, with exponents reduced modulo $7$, are
\[
\sigma^0(\eta)=\zeta+\zeta^6,\quad
\sigma^1(\eta)=\zeta^3+\zeta^4,\quad
\sigma^2(\eta)=\zeta^2+\zeta^5,
\]
\[
\sigma^3(\eta)=\zeta^6+\zeta,\quad
\sigma^4(\eta)=\zeta^4+\zeta^3,\quad
\sigma^5(\eta)=\zeta^5+\zeta^2.
\]
The rule behind them is $\sigma^k(\zeta)=\zeta^{3^k}$ with $3^k$ then reduced modulo $7$: $3^3=27\equiv 6$, so $\sigma^3(\zeta)=\zeta^6$, and $3^5=243\equiv 5$, with $-243\equiv 2$. Since $\sigma$ fixes $\QQ$ and respects inverses, $\sigma^k(\eta)=\sigma^k(\zeta)+(\sigma^k(\zeta))^{-1}$.

The list collapses to three elements, each occurring twice: $\eta=\zeta+\zeta^6$ at $k=0,3$; $\zeta^3+\zeta^4$ at $k=1,4$; $\zeta^2+\zeta^5$ at $k=2,5$. So the stabilizer of $\eta$ in $G$ is $\{\sigma^0,\sigma^3\}$, of order $2$, and $[\QQ(\eta):\QQ]=6/2=3$. The three distinct values are the conjugates of $\eta$.

The three conjugates between them contain each of $\zeta,\zeta^2,\dots,\zeta^6$ exactly once, so
\[
\eta+\sigma(\eta)+\sigma^2(\eta)=\zeta+\zeta^2+\cdots+\zeta^6=-1,
\]
because $\zeta$ is a root of $\Phi_7=x^6+\cdots+x+1$ and $\zeta\ne1$. The sum of the roots is $-1$, so the coefficient of $x^2$ in the cubic is $1$.

The first pairwise product is
\[
\eta\,\sigma(\eta)=(\zeta+\zeta^6)(\zeta^3+\zeta^4)=\zeta^4+\zeta^5+\zeta^9+\zeta^{10}=\zeta^2+\zeta^3+\zeta^4+\zeta^5,
\]
four distinct powers, so no terms combine.

The second is
\[
\eta\,\sigma^2(\eta)=(\zeta+\zeta^6)(\zeta^2+\zeta^5)=\zeta^3+\zeta^6+\zeta^{8}+\zeta^{11}=\zeta+\zeta^3+\zeta^4+\zeta^6,
\]
and the third is
\[
\sigma(\eta)\,\sigma^2(\eta)=(\zeta^3+\zeta^4)(\zeta^2+\zeta^5)=\zeta^5+\zeta^{8}+\zeta^{6}+\zeta^{9}=\zeta+\zeta^2+\zeta^5+\zeta^6.
\]
Adding the three products term by term, each of $\zeta,\zeta^2,\dots,\zeta^6$ occurs exactly twice, so
\[
\eta\sigma(\eta)+\eta\sigma^2(\eta)+\sigma(\eta)\sigma^2(\eta)=2(\zeta+\zeta^2+\cdots+\zeta^6)=-2,
\]
and the coefficient of $x$ is $-2$.

The product of all three is
\[
\eta\eta_1\eta_2=(\zeta+\zeta^{6})(\zeta^{5}+\zeta+\zeta^{6}+\zeta^{2})
=\zeta^{6}+\zeta^{2}+1+\zeta^{3}+\zeta^{4}+1+\zeta^{5}+\zeta
=2+(\zeta+\zeta^{2}+\cdots+\zeta^{6})=2-1=1.
\]
Two of those eight products are $\zeta^{7}=1$; the other six are exactly $\zeta,\zeta^{2},\dots,\zeta^{6}$, whose sum is $-1$. So the product is $1$ and the constant term is $-1$. The minimal polynomial is therefore
\[
x^{3}-(\eta+\sigma(\eta)+\sigma^{2}(\eta))x^{2}+(\eta\sigma(\eta)+\eta\sigma^{2}(\eta)+\sigma(\eta)\sigma^{2}(\eta))x-\eta\sigma(\eta)\sigma^{2}(\eta)
=x^{3}+x^{2}-2x-1.
\]

It remains to show that $\QQ(\eta)$ is the only cubic subfield. Let $L\subseteq K$ be any subfield with $[L:\QQ]=3$, and let $N$ be the subgroup of $G$ consisting of the automorphisms that fix every element of $L$. The Galois correspondence gives $[G:N]=[L:\QQ]=3$, so $|N|=|G|/3=2$. A cyclic group of order $6$ has exactly one subgroup of order $2$, namely $H=\langle\sigma_6\rangle=\{\sigma_1,\sigma_6\}$, so $N=H$. Applying the correspondence once more, $L=K^N=K^H=\QQ(\eta)$. Hence every cubic subfield of $K$ equals $\QQ(\eta)$, and that subfield is unique.
% ===== END SOLUTION problem-14c =====

\begin{problem}{problem-14c-order-two}
The subgroup of order 2 in $G=\operatorname{Gal}(\QQ(\zeta)/\QQ)$ is $H=\langle\sigma_6\rangle=\{\sigma_1,\sigma_6\}$, where $\sigma_k(\zeta)=\zeta^k$ and exponents are read modulo 7.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION problem-14c-order-two =====
Since $\sigma_6^2=\sigma_{36}=\sigma_1$ and $\sigma_6\ne\sigma_1$, the element $\sigma_6$ has order 2, and the subgroup it generates is $\{\sigma_1,\sigma_6\}$, the identity included. The identity is $\sigma_1$, not $\sigma_3$: $\sigma_3$ has order 6, because $\sigma_3^2=\sigma_9=\sigma_2$ and $\sigma_3^3=\sigma_{27}=\sigma_6$, so $\sigma_3$ generates all of $G$. The other subgroup of order 3 is $\langle\sigma_2\rangle=\{\sigma_1,\sigma_2,\sigma_4\}$, which is not $H$.

The subgroup $H$ is the only subgroup of order 2. A cyclic group of order 6 has exactly one subgroup of each order dividing 6, and the Galois correspondence between subgroups of $G$ and intermediate fields of $\QQ(\zeta)/\QQ$ is one-to-one and inclusion-reversing. So $H$ fixes a unique subfield of degree $6/2=3$ over $\QQ$, namely $K=\QQ(\zeta)^H$.
% ===== END SOLUTION problem-14c-order-two =====
