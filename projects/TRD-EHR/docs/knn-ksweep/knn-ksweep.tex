\begin{problem}{1}
With plain cosine similarity, weights $w_j=\max(s_j,0)^{\alpha}$ ($\alpha=1$) and risk score
$R_k=\sum_{j\le k} w_j y_j/\sum_{j\le k} w_j$, the full-pool weights are nearly uniform
(similarities $0.83$--$0.95$; effective sample size $34{,}032$ of $34{,}063$).
Why does the test-set ROC AUC at the whole pool end at $0.608$ rather than falling to $0.5$?
\end{problem}
\begin{solution}
% the student's argument, from live/answers/t0023-r4.png
The weights of the nearer neighbours, who should have more influence, are still larger.
That is not the case with exactly uniform weighting. The effect is stronger with
importance-weighted cosine similarity.
\end{solution}
