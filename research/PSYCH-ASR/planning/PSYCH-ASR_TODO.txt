TURN-RESOLUTION PARAMETERS — INVESTIGATION DONE, EXPERIMENT NOT (Added 2026-08-17.) This
answers the loose note that used to sit at the top of the retired run_whisperx.py ("parameter to
decrease time of a cut between interchanges if there is a lot of interrupting"). The note
is deleted; the answer lives here and in README.md's "Turn-resolution knobs" subsection.
There IS such a parameter on each side of the pipeline, they live in different libraries,
and the instinct points at the WRONG side:
  * DIARIZATION SIDE — segmentation.min_duration_off, ALREADY MAXED OUT. It is pyannote
    SpeakerDiarization's ONLY timing hyperparameter, and it FILLS intra-speaker gaps shorter
    than N seconds, MERGING two turns into one. community-1's config.yaml sets it to 0.0 —
    no filling, every detected pause already becomes a turn boundary. The knob only runs in
    the merge direction and there is nothing below zero. Turn resolution here is a property
    of the powerset segmentation model, not a setting. clustering.threshold (0.6), Fa (0.07)
    and Fb (0.8) decide WHO a turn belongs to, not WHERE it is cut. Do not go looking again.
  * ASR SIDE — chunk_size, DEFAULT 30 s, AND THIS IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS. WhisperX runs its
    own bundled VAD first and merges consecutive speech regions into decode chunks until the
    span exceeds chunk_size (whisperx/vads/vad.py, merge_chunks). Under heavy interruption
    one 30 s chunk spans many speaker changes, Whisper decodes it as a single block, and its
    segments straddle speaker boundaries. Forced alignment re-splitting at SENTENCE
    boundaries softens that but cannot fix it — a sentence boundary is not a speaker
    boundary, and a sentence two people built together has neither. chunk_size, vad_onset
    (0.500) and vad_offset (0.363) are all settable via the vad_options dict argument of
    whisperx.load_model. psych_asr/asr/align.py passes nothing, so all three are at defaults.
  * NOT REACHABLE through vad_options: the VAD's own min_duration_on / min_duration_off,
    hardcoded to 0.1 s in whisperx/vads/pyannote.py's load_vad_model. Changing them means
    building the VAD pipeline yourself and handing it to load_model as vad_model, which the
    loader accepts and which then overrides vad_method.
  * OVERRIDING A PYANNOTE PARAM, if ever needed: DiarizationPipeline keeps the pyannote
    Pipeline on its .model attribute, and that object's instantiate method takes a PARTIAL
    nested dict — segmentation is itself a sub-pipeline, so passing only its sub-dict resets
    that one value and leaves clustering as the config set it. Do this rather than editing
    the staged config.yaml; the staged model dir stays a faithful copy of the download.
[ ] THE EXPERIMENT, when the overlap recording lands: run it twice — once at the default
    chunk_size and once shorter (15 s is the obvious first probe) — and score both against
    the SAME persisted turn table. Shorter chunks cut more often and cost Whisper decoding
    context, so this is a real WER-vs-boundary trade, not a free win. That is exactly why it
    is an experiment and not a default to change on argument. Nothing to do until the audio

FOUR ROUTES TO A GROUND METRIC. They cost different amounts and answer different
questions. ONLY THE LAST ONE SCORES THE ARMS. The first three are still worth doing,
because two of them run TODAY against no audio at all and one of them validates the
instrument every other number depends on.

R1 - CALIBRATE THE SCORER BY INJECTING KNOWN ERRORS.
     This is the literal reading of the todo line "reconstruct true file with errors to use
     as a ground metric". score_arms.py's only verification today is scoring the baseline
     arm against ITSELF: 0.00% DER at both collars, 0.0% ratio error, 18/18 backchannels.
     That proves the identity case and nothing else. It does not show that the
     decomposition puts boundary error in the right bucket, that a swap is attributed to
     speaker confusion rather than to missed speech plus false alarm, or that the collar
     behaves the way this file and README.md both claim it does.
     THE INSTRUMENT: take a turn table, treat it as the reference, and derive hypotheses
     from it by applying ONE controlled corruption at a time at a KNOWN magnitude —
     boundary jitter of plus/minus N ms on every turn edge; a speaker swap across a window
     of known duration; deletion of turns totalling a known number of seconds; insertion of
     false-alarm turns of known duration; a global time shift. Then check that score_arms.py
     recovers each injected quantity in the expected COMPONENT and at the expected SIZE,
     and that collar 0.25s absorbs jitter below the collar while collar 0s does not.
     IT DOES NOT NEED A REAL TURN TABLE AT ALL, AND SHOULD NOT USE ONE. A synthetic
     two-speaker turn table generated from a random alternating process is an identical
     instrument test and touches no session data. Build it that way and the entire route
     runs outside the fence, reproducibly, with a fixed seed, on a laptop's worth of CPU.
     WHAT IT ESTABLISHES: that the number the bake-off will be decided on measures what it
     says it measures. WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH: anything whatsoever about the arms.

R2 - SYNTHETIC OVERLAP, WITH GROUND TRUTH BY CONSTRUCTION.
     The bake-off is stuck because session 1 carries 0.5-1.5% overlap and every arm agrees
     with every other on 99.3-99.7% of words, and the recording that would separate them
     belongs to a collaborator and has not arrived. A controlled two-speaker mixture
     supplies the missing axis without waiting on anyone: take two NON-PHI speakers (a
     public read-speech corpus, or synthesized speech), lay them on a timeline with a KNOWN
     turn structure, and mix at a swept overlap fraction — 0%, 5%, 10%, 20%, 30%. The
     reference is written by the mixer rather than by a human, so DER is exact and free at
     every point on the sweep, and the sweep can be as long as convenient.
     WHAT IT ESTABLISHES: each arm's DER as a FUNCTION of overlap, which is precisely the
     claim the three challengers exist to make and precisely the claim session 1 cannot
     test. It also answers open question 2 of the bake-off section — whether an end-to-end
     model survives a long file — at any duration, and it exercises arm B's window stitcher
     against a seam whose truth is known, which is the only way to separate ITS seam error
     from Sortformer's model error.
     WHAT IT DOES NOT ESTABLISH, AND THIS HAS TO BE STATED EVERY TIME THE NUMBER IS QUOTED:
     it is not therapy audio. One room, one microphone, real reverberation, backchannel
     prosody, disfluency, and the register of a clinical conversation are not reproduced by
     mixing read speech. Treat it as a SCREEN and a FALSIFIER — an arm that fails on clean
     synthetic overlap will not do better on the real recording — never as the headline.
     DECISIONS THE USER OWNS HERE, EACH WITH A DEFENSIBLE ALTERNATIVE: which source speech;
     what the overlap grid is; whether the mix is additive at a fixed SNR or convolved with
     a room impulse response first; and whether the synthetic turn-length distribution is
     fitted to session 1's or chosen independently. Every one of them changes what the
     curve means.

R3 - REFERENCE-FREE, AND IT SETTLES ONE STANDING QUESTION CHEAPLY.
     Two measurements need no ground truth at all, and both are assistant-ownable end to
     end: turn tables in, numbers out, nothing rendered.
     [ ] SELF-CONSISTENCY UNDER PERTURBATION. Re-run each arm on the same audio under a
         perturbation that MUST NOT change the answer — a sub-second time shift, a small
         gain change, a different seed where one exists — and score each arm against its
         own first run with the same DER machinery. An arm that disagrees with ITSELF is
         unreliable regardless of where the truth lies, and the number is comparable across
         arms without anybody knowing the truth. Cheap: five arms, one extra 1b pass each.
     [ ] CONSENSUS AS A STATED-BIAS PSEUDO-REFERENCE. Score each arm against the majority
         vote of the OTHER arms. This is NOT accuracy — four arms can agree and all be
         wrong, and the warning under ADJUDICATING THE ARMS applies here verbatim — but it
         identifies the OUTLIER without a human, which is exactly the standing arm C
         question: its roughly 190s of extra covered speech scored as false alarm against
         the BASELINE alone, and the baseline is not truth either. Against the consensus of
         three other arms that reading is stronger. It is still not proof, and it must
         never be reported as DER.

R4 - THE HAND-CORRECTED REFERENCE. STILL THE ONLY THING THAT SCORES THE ARMS.

     annotated the whole of session 1 against the baseline arm's transcript in a

     WHAT THE REFERENCE DOES NOT HAVE, AND WHY THE REST OF THIS ITEM SURVIVES: word
     timings. A corrected turn's span is the host turn's own, split proportionally to
     character offset; an inserted turn has zero duration. It is the corrected words and
     the corrected turn sequence, which is enough to compare attribution and turn structure
     and is NOT enough for DER. The reference RTTM needs the corrected words re-aligned to

     [x] THE CORRECTION WORKSPACE. Done, and it is a spreadsheet the human already knows

         now the thing standing between the reference and a DER, and it is a GPU job.
     [ ] THE UEM WRITER. Already a standing item in the bake-off section and it belongs
         here: the corrected subset defines the evaluation region, and pyannote.metrics
         without a UEM approximates that region as the union of reference and hypothesis
         extents, scoring each arm over audio the reference never annotated. The UEM falls
         out mechanically from which spans were corrected — it is bookkeeping, not judgement.
     [ ] A REFERENCE VALIDATOR that runs before anything is scored: no overlapping turns
         within a single speaker, no zero or negative durations, no turn past the end of
         the audio, labels drawn from a closed set, and total annotated duration matching
         the UEM. A malformed reference yields a DER that is WRONG rather than an ERROR,
         which is the worst failure mode available anywhere in this project.
     [ ] THE SUMMARY REPORT for the corrected subset — spans corrected, total corrected

     corrected as a subset instead:
     * WHICH SPANS GET CORRECTED. Correcting only the 57 arm-disagreement regions measures
       where the arms DIFFER, not where they are all WRONG, and biases every DER downward
       in a way no amount of scoring discipline recovers afterwards. A defensible frame has
       at least three strata — the disagreement regions; unanimous-but-low-confidence spans
       (avg_logprob, and no_speech_prob once it is retained); and a uniform random spine
       that is conditioned on NO model's output — but the strata, their sampling rates and
       the total minute budget are an estimand decision.
     * HOW THE STRATA ARE WEIGHTED BACK TOGETHER. If they are sampled at different rates
       then the session-level DER is a weighted estimate and the POOLED number is not it.
       Reporting the pooled number is reporting DER-on-the-hard-parts under a session-level
       label, which is a worse error than reporting nothing.
     * WHETHER ONE SESSION'S SUBSET IS A TEST SET AT ALL, or whether the reference must
       span sessions before any arm is declared the winner. Session 1 is the didactic intro
       and is not representative of the corpus on any axis that matters here.

STEP 2 — THE LLM, AND ONLY ON THE REGIONS WHERE THE ARMS DISAGREE.
  The judgement a model can actually make here, and that the diff cannot, is DISCOURSE
  COHERENCE: given the same words segmented two different ways, which reading works as a
  conversation between two people? That is a pragmatics question, which is exactly what an
  instruct model is good at and exactly what a timestamp diff cannot touch.
  [ ] UNIT = ONE DISAGREEMENT REGION IN CONTEXT, never a whole transcript. Same reasoning
      as Stage 3c's turn unit: a whole-session verdict is one unverifiable impression,
      while a few hundred small region-level judgements are individually checkable and
      aggregate with a known composition. compare_arms.py already emits each region with
      ±25 words of context for this purpose.
  [ ] PRESENT THE CANDIDATE LABELINGS BLIND AND IN RANDOMIZED ORDER — "Version A" /
      "Version B", never "pyannote" / "Sortformer". A model that knows which vendor it is
      looking at will answer from its prior about the vendor. Record the A/B-to-arm mapping
      on the client side and un-blind after the response comes back.
  [ ] ASK FOR STRUCTURED OUTPUT: which version reads as coherent dialogue, a confidence,
      and ONE sentence of reason. Ollama's /api/chat takes a `format` JSON schema, which
      constrains decoding server-side — same discipline as Stage 3c's guided JSON, no
      fence-stripping on the client.
  [ ] CACHE PER REGION in a small SQLite db keyed by (stem, region index, arm pair, model)
      so a re-run resumes nearly free. Same pattern Stage 3c will reuse at scale; this is
      the cheap place to get it right first.
  [ ] AGGREGATE AS PAIRWISE WIN RATES, not as a score. "Arm C read more coherently than
      the baseline in 61 of 88 disputed regions" is a defensible sentence. "Arm C scored
      7.4" is not.

SERVING — REUSE ~/libr-local-llm, DO NOT BUILD ANYTHING NEW.
  `ollama-up single` submits the server and refuses to report success unless it is actually
  listening; the endpoint is LOOPBACK ON THE JOB'S NODE (127.0.0.1:11500), reachable by
  stepping onto that node with `srun --jobid=<id> --overlap`. Do NOT rebind to 0.0.0.0 —
  that exposes a PHI-processing endpoint to every user on the cluster.
  HARD CONSTRAINT, NOT A PREFERENCE: whatever drives this must have NO TOOL-CALLING
  SURFACE. That disqualifies `ollama-code` / opencode for this job outright, however
  convenient it is — a tool-enabled agent putting a session fragment into a search query is
  an exfiltration event under the on-prem constraint, not a bug. Write a plain Python HTTP
  client against /api/chat. Nothing else.
  MODEL: start with medgemma:27b-it-q8_0 (resident, q8 rather than q4 because quantization
  damage shows up first on careful text work). qwen3-coder:30b is the wrong tool — it is
  tuned for agentic coding. gpt-oss:120b would likely judge best but needs c3_accel, which
  is the single 4-GPU node and is also where the bake-off's own GPU jobs run. Same open
  decision as Stage 3c's model choice, and it should be settled the same way: by measuring
  agreement against a human on a handful of regions, not by argument.

WHAT THIS IS NOT, AND THIS MATTERS MORE THAN THE REST OF THE SECTION:
  * IT IS NOT A DER SUBSTITUTE. Agreement between arms is not accuracy — four arms can
    agree and all be wrong, and a model's preference between two labelings is not evidence
    about which matches the audio. The hand-corrected reference RTTM from Stage 2 is what
    scores the arms. FULL STOP.
  * IT IS A TRIAGE TOOL. Its output is a ranked list of WHERE TO LISTEN, which is what
    makes the human hand-correction pass affordable — and the human pass is the thing that
    actually produces the reference.
  * DO NOT REPORT LLM PREFERENCE AS A RESULT anywhere a reviewer will read it, unless it
    has itself been validated against human judgement on a subset. The project's whole
    posture is "quantify the quality, don't assume it," and that applies to the judge as
    much as to the thing being judged.

LATER — STAGE 3b: ACOUSTIC / PARALINGUISTIC (raw waveform)
================================================================
(Added 2026-08-13, from deck questions 1 and 4.) This lane reads the RAW WAVEFORM and uses
the transcript only to say WHERE TO LOOK. That decoupling is what makes it robust: a
misrecognized word still has correct BOUNDARIES, because forced alignment placed those
boundaries acoustically.

[ ] ONE ACOUSTIC PASS PER SESSION, aggregated afterwards at whatever granularity a feature
    needs. Tool: the opensmile python package (binaries bundled, no network at runtime) in
    LOW-LEVEL-DESCRIPTOR mode with the eGeMAPSv02 set — a frame-level table at a 10 ms hop
    covering F0, loudness, jitter, shimmer, harmonics-to-noise ratio, formants, and spectral
    descriptors. Every word / turn / session feature is then a windowed aggregate of that ONE
    table, sliced by timestamps we already have. Prefer eGeMAPS over a hand-rolled feature
    set specifically because it is the STANDARD set in computational paralinguistics — a
    reviewer recognizes it and it needs no defending in a methods section.
[ ] Where a genuinely word-scoped pitch CONTOUR is wanted (rising terminal, emphatic peak):
    praat-parselmouth, which gives Praat's own F0 and intensity estimation from python and is
    the reference implementation in speech science.
[ ] Z-SCORE WITHIN SPEAKER WITHIN SESSION before any cross-speaker comparison. See DECISIONS
    LOCKED. Non-negotiable.
[ ] DIMENSIONAL AFFECT PER TURN: audeering/wav2vec2-large-robust-12-ft-emotion-msp-dim maps a
    speech span to continuous AROUSAL / DOMINANCE / VALENCE rather than discrete emotion
    labels. Continuous is the right choice — therapy affect does not sort into
    angry/sad/happy, and three numbers per turn COMPOSE with the rest of the feature table
    where a categorical label does not. Stage it offline like everything else.
    INTEGRATION TRAP: this checkpoint is NOT a plain AutoModelForAudioClassification. It uses
    a custom regression head defined on the model card, which has to be reproduced locally
    for the weights to load.
[ ] NON-VERBAL VOCAL EVENTS — sighs, huffs, breaths, laughter, throat clearing. Deck question
    4, and a real feature rather than a curiosity: an audible sigh is exactly the moment that
    would justify a therapist pivoting to grounding or relaxation, and it is INVISIBLE in a
    transcript. Tool: an AudioSet-pretrained audio-event classifier — the ontology contains
    precisely the categories at issue (Sigh, Gasp, Breathing, Groan, Grunt, Throat clearing,
    Sniff, Cough, Laughter, Crying/sobbing). Checkpoint:
    MIT/ast-finetuned-audioset-10-10-0.4593, available through transformers as
    ASTForAudioClassification, stages offline exactly like the ASR and diarization weights.
    TWO DESIGN POINTS DO MOST OF THE WORK:
      * RUN IT ON THE GAPS, NOT ON THE SPEECH. The word timestamps and turn table already
        partition the session. Sighs and breaths live in the silences — between turns, and in
        intra-turn gaps longer than roughly 400 ms. Classifying only those windows cuts the
        compute by roughly the talk-time fraction AND removes the dominant false-positive
        source, which is the classifier firing on ordinary speech.
      * ATTRIBUTE THE EVENT TO A SPEAKER. An event in a gap belongs to whoever is speaking on
        either side of it; when the flanking speakers DIFFER the attribution is genuinely
        ambiguous and must be RECORDED AS SUCH rather than guessed. A sigh is a different
        feature depending on whose it is.
[ ] VALIDATE THE EVENT DETECTOR BEFORE TRUSTING ANY FEATURE BUILT ON IT. AudioSet models are
    trained on consumer video audio; precision on 16 kHz mono clinical recordings is UNKNOWN.
    Needs a small hand-labelled validation set — the same "quantify the quality, don't assume
    it" discipline the ASR side already runs on.
