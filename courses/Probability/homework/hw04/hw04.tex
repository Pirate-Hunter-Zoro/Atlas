\begin{problem}{2}
Whether or not it rains today depends on the weather of the last three days. Show how this system can be analyzed as a Markov chain. How many states are needed?
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION 2 =====
Write the weather day by day, for instance rain, sun, rain, sun, sun. Combine the last three days into a single state. Each day is rain or sun, so there are $2^3 = 8$ possible states.
% ===== END SOLUTION 2 =====

\begin{problem}{3}
In Exercise 2, suppose that if it has rained for the past three days, then it will rain today with probability 0.8; if it did not rain for any of the past three days, then it will rain today with probability 0.2; and in any other case the weather today will, with probability 0.6, be the same as the weather yesterday. Determine $P$ for this Markov chain.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION 3 =====
Label the eight states, oldest day first, and index them $0$ to $7$ in that order:

$$\begin{gathered} 0:(S,S,S),\quad 1:(S,S,R),\quad 2:(S,R,S),\quad 3:(S,R,R), \\ 4:(R,S,S),\quad 5:(R,S,R),\quad 6:(R,R,S),\quad 7:(R,R,R). \end{gathered}$$

Then

$$P = \begin{pmatrix}
.8 & .2 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & .4 & .6 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & .6 & .4 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & .4 & .6 \\
.6 & .4 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & .4 & .6 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & .6 & .4 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & .2 & .8
\end{pmatrix}.$$
% ===== END SOLUTION 3 =====

\begin{problem}{5}
A Markov chain $\{X_n,\ n\ge 0\}$ with states $0,1,2$ has transition probability matrix

$$P=\begin{pmatrix} \tfrac12 & \tfrac13 & \tfrac16 \\[2pt] 0 & \tfrac13 & \tfrac23 \\[2pt] \tfrac12 & 0 & \tfrac12 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad p=\left(\tfrac14,\ \tfrac14,\ \tfrac12\right),$$

where $p$ is the initial distribution, the third entry following from $P\{X_0=0\}=P\{X_0=1\}=\tfrac14$. Find $E[X_3]$.
\end{problem}
% ===== SOLUTION 5 =====
Every row of $P$ sums to $1$, so $P$ is a transition matrix and $p$ is a distribution. The distribution of $X_3$ is $pP^3$: a $1\times n$ vector times an $n\times n$ matrix three times, which gives a $1\times n$ vector again.

$$P^2 = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac13 & \tfrac5{18} & \tfrac7{18} \\[2pt] \tfrac13 & \tfrac19 & \tfrac59 \\[2pt] \tfrac12 & \tfrac16 & \tfrac13 \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
P^3 = \begin{pmatrix} \tfrac{13}{36} & \tfrac{11}{54} & \tfrac{47}{108} \\[2pt] \tfrac49 & \tfrac4{27} & \tfrac{11}{27} \\[2pt] \tfrac5{12} & \tfrac29 & \tfrac{13}{36} \end{pmatrix}.$$

Then

$$pP^3 = \left(\tfrac{59}{144},\ \tfrac{43}{216},\ \tfrac{169}{432}\right),$$

whose entries sum to $1$, and for a state taking the value $j$ with probability $P\{X_3=j\}$,

$$E[X_3] = P\{X_3=1\} + 2P\{X_3=2\} = \tfrac{43}{216} + 2\cdot\tfrac{169}{432} = \tfrac{53}{54}.$$
% ===== END SOLUTION 5 =====
