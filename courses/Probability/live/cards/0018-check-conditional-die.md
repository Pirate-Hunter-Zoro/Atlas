Before Ross 39, one turn of the handle so the definition is automatic rather
than something you are reading off a card.

One roll of a fair die. Let

$$B = \{4,5,6\} \quad (\text{the roll is at least } 4), \qquad
A = \{1,3,5\} \quad (\text{the roll is odd}).$$

Compute $P(A \mid B)$.

Write the three things: the set $AB$, the two probabilities $P(AB)$ and $P(B)$,
and the quotient. Then say in one clause whether being told the roll is at least
$4$ made *odd* more likely or less likely than it was.
