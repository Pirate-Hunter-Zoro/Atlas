title: Multiplying by 0.7 made the number bigger
---

You have a number now, and the route to it is sound: $1/9 + 1/5 = 14/45$ is
right, and cancelling the common $45$ at the end is right. One term is not.

Look at what you did to $0.7 \cdot \frac{4}{9}$. You wrote $\frac{28}{45}$.

$$\frac{28}{45} \approx 0.62, \qquad \frac{4}{9} \approx 0.44.$$

Multiplying by $0.7$ has to make a positive number **smaller**, and yours came
out larger. Whatever happened between those two fractions, it was not
multiplication by $0.7$.

The same wrong term sits in the numerator and in the denominator, which is why
the page still looks consistent — but the denominator has two other terms in it,
so the error does not cancel and $2/3$ is not the answer.

---

Compute $0.7 \cdot \frac{4}{9}$ on its own, as a fraction times a fraction, and
put the corrected value in both places. Then divide again.
