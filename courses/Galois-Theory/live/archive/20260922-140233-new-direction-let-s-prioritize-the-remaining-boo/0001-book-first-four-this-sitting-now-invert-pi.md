**Exercise 4.9.** *Suppose that $a$ and $b$ are complex numbers which are
transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$. Is $ab$ transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$?*

Answer yes or no and prove it. A single counterexample settles a no.

Everything 4.9 uses:

- $\mathbb{Q}$ — the rational numbers. Here $a$ and $b$ lie in $\mathbb{C}$, and the extension in play is $\mathbb{C} : \mathbb{Q}$.
- $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ — the polynomials in $x$ with rational coefficients.
- **algebraic over $K$** — a root of some polynomial over $K$ that is not the zero polynomial.
- **transcendental over $K$** — not algebraic over $K$: no non-zero polynomial over $K$ has it as a root.
- $ab$ — the ordinary product in $\mathbb{C}$.
- $\pi$ — transcendental over $\mathbb{Q}$ (Lindemann, 1882). You may quote that.
- **counterexample** — one pair $a, b$, both transcendental, whose product is algebraic.

**Your move.** Not 4.9 itself — one small fact it will need. Suppose $1/\pi$ were
algebraic over $\mathbb{Q}$, a root of the non-zero polynomial
$c_0 + c_1 x + \cdots + c_n x^n$ with rational $c_i$, not all zero. Write down a
non-zero polynomial with rational coefficients having $\pi$ as a root. One line,
built out of those same coefficients.
