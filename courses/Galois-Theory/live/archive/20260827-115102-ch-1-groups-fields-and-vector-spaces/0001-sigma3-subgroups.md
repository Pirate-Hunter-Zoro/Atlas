## The plan for §1.1

Three exercises from the end of the section, in this order. Not all ten — these three are the ones that force the section's ideas to be *used* rather than recited.

- **1.3** — a subgroup of index $2$ is normal. First, because it makes you handle cosets, the index and normality together, and the rest of §1.1 is machinery for it.
- **1.7** — a group is generated by each of its non-identity elements if and only if it is finite cyclic of prime order. This is where Lagrange's theorem and the order of an element have to work in the same argument.
- **1.5** — is normality transitive? Last, because it is the sharp corner, and Garling has already put the counterexample on the page a paragraph earlier.

Left out on purpose, not by accident: 1.1 and 1.6 are routine, 1.2 is bookkeeping you will redo in Ch 3, 1.4 is a near-twin of 1.3, and 1.8–1.10 are variations on 1.7 and on the sign homomorphism. We can come back to any of them.

## What a coset is

Let $H$ be a subgroup of $G$ and let $g \in G$. Garling works with **right** cosets throughout:

$$H \circ g = \{\, h \circ g \;:\; h \in H \,\}.$$

A coset is *not* a subgroup — it is $H$ shoved along by $g$. Three facts, and they are the whole toolkit for 1.3:

1. $|H \circ g| = |H|$, because $h \mapsto h \circ g$ is a bijection from $H$ onto $H \circ g$.
2. Distinct right cosets are **disjoint**, so the cosets partition $G$. The number of them is the index $|G/H|$, and $|G| = |G/H| \cdot |H|$ — Lagrange.
3. $g \in H \circ g$ always (take $h = e$). So $H \circ g = H$ **exactly when** $g \in H$, and $H \circ g$ is one of the *other* cosets exactly when $g \notin H$.

And normality, in the form Garling defines it: $H \lhd G$ means $g^{-1} \circ h \circ g \in H$ for every $g \in G$ and every $h \in H$.

## Watch it happen in $\Sigma_3$

$\Sigma_3$ is the six permutations of $\{1,2,3\}$: $e$, $(12)$, $(13)$, $(23)$, $(123)$, $(132)$. Composition is **right to left** — $\sigma \circ \tau$ applies $\tau$ first.

Take $H = \{e, (12)\}$, so $|H| = 2$ and the index is $6/2 = 3$. Its right cosets:

| $g$ | $H \circ g$ |
|---|---|
| $e$ | $\{e,\ (12)\}$ |
| $(13)$ | $\{(13),\ (132)\}$ |
| $(23)$ | $\{(23),\ (123)\}$ |

Three cosets, two elements each, six elements of $\Sigma_3$ accounted for exactly once. (Check one by hand: $(12) \circ (13)$ sends $1 \mapsto 3$, $3 \mapsto 2$, $2 \mapsto 1$, which is $(132)$.)

Is this $H$ normal? Test $g = (13)$, $h = (12)$. Since $(13)^{-1} = (13)$,

$$(13) \circ (12) \circ (13) = (13) \circ (132) = (23),$$

and $(23) \notin H$. So $H$ is **not** normal in $\Sigma_3$. Index $3$ bought us nothing.

Index $2$ is different, and that is the point of the exercise: if $|G/H| = 2$ there are exactly **two** right cosets, and you already know what one of them is.

## Your exercise — Garling 1.3

> Suppose that $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ of index $2$. Show that $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$.

Use Lagrange and the three coset facts above freely; nothing else is needed, and no assumption that $G$ is finite is needed either.
