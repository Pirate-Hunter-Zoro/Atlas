**Exercise 6.5.** Show that the point $(2^{1/3},0)$ is not constructible. This is the impossibility of *duplicating the cube* --- building, with ruler and compass alone, a cube of twice the volume of a given one.

You are not answering that yet. Everything it uses, first:

- **Constructible point** --- a point $P$ for which there is a finite list $P_0,P_1,\dots,P_n=P$, starting from $P_0=(0,0)$ and $P_1=(1,0)$, in which every later point is an intersection of two lines, or of a line and a circle, or of two circles, all drawn from points already in the list.
- **$\mathbb{Q}(x,y)$** --- the smallest subfield of $\mathbb{R}$ containing $\mathbb{Q}$ and both $x$ and $y$.
- **$[L:K]$** --- the dimension of the field $L$ as a vector space over its subfield $K$.
- **Theorem 6.1** --- if $(x,y)$ is a constructible point, then $[\mathbb{Q}(x,y):\mathbb{Q}]=2^{r}$ for some integer $r\ge 0$.
- **Minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$** --- the monic polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ of least degree having $\alpha$ as a root.
- **Theorem 4.4** --- $[\mathbb{Q}(\alpha):\mathbb{Q}]$ equals the degree of the minimal polynomial of $\alpha$ over $\mathbb{Q}$.
- **Eisenstein's criterion** --- if a prime $p$ divides every coefficient of $f$ except the leading one, and $p^{2}$ does not divide the constant term, then $f$ is irreducible over $\mathbb{Q}$.

Here is the one rung. All three of these polynomials have $2^{1/3}$ as a root:

$$x^{6}-4 \qquad\qquad x^{3}-2 \qquad\qquad x^{9}-8$$

Which one is the minimal polynomial of $2^{1/3}$ over $\mathbb{Q}$, and what disqualifies the other two?
