Next is 10(d), the last problem on the sheet. You already own both of its main pieces: roots go to roots, and an automorphism is determined by what it does to generators. The new part is turning that into a map to $S_3$ (the group of permutations of three things).

**10(d).** Let $E = \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[3]{2}, \zeta_3)$. Show that every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ permutes the three roots of $x^3 - 2$ and is determined by that permutation. Conclude that $\operatorname{Aut}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_3$.

What you need:
- $\omega = \zeta_3 = e^{2\pi i/3}$.
- The roots of $x^3 - 2$ are $r_1 = \sqrt[3]{2}$, $r_2 = \omega\sqrt[3]{2}$, $r_3 = \omega^2\sqrt[3]{2}$, pairwise distinct (10(a)).
- $E = \mathbb{Q}(r_1, r_2, r_3)$: $E$ is generated by the three roots (10(a)).
- $\operatorname{Aut}(E/\mathbb{Q})$: the field automorphisms of $E$ that fix every rational. It is a group under composition.
- Roots go to roots: if $f \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ and $f(r) = 0$, then $f(\sigma(r)) = 0$.
- An automorphism of $\mathbb{Q}(r_1, r_2, r_3)$ fixing $\mathbb{Q}$ is determined by $\sigma(r_1), \sigma(r_2), \sigma(r_3)$.
- $\sigma$ is injective, since it is a field homomorphism.
- A homomorphism of groups is injective exactly when its kernel is trivial; then the group is isomorphic to its image, which is a subgroup.

Show every $\sigma \in \operatorname{Aut}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ permutes $\{r_1, r_2, r_3\}$ and is determined by that permutation. Then conclude $\operatorname{Aut}(E/\mathbb{Q})$ is isomorphic to a subgroup of $S_3$.
