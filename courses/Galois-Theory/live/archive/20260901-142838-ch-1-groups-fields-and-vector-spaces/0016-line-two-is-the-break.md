Line 1 is right, and it is the part people get wrong: $g^{-1} = (1\,3\,2)$. Reversing a cycle by reading it backwards is correct.

The break is line 2, where you collapsed

$$(1\,2)(1\,3\,2) \;=\; (3\,1\,2).$$

That is a 3-cycle. The two things you multiplied cannot produce one — but I want you to see it rather than take it from me, so do this instead of taking my word.

**Track a single point.** Take $1$. Push it through $(1\,3\,2)$, then through $(1\,2)$, and write down where it lands. Then ask your answer $(3\,1\,2)$ where $1$ goes; it says $2$.

One thing you have not committed to, and it is why the line went sideways: **which factor acts first.** A product $\sigma\tau$ means one of two things — apply $\tau$ then $\sigma$ (right to left, the usual convention, since these are functions), or apply $\sigma$ then $\tau$. Pick one, write it at the top of the page, and use it for every line. Either convention gives a consistent answer; mixing them gives yours.

Once the element is right, the order follows immediately and we will take it then.

**Your move:** state your convention in one line, then track $1$ and $3$ through $(1\,2)(1\,3\,2)$ under it, and write the resulting element as a cycle.
