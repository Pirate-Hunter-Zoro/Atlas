**§1.1, the set I have chosen:** **1.3**, then **1.7**, then **1.5**.
1.3 because it makes you *use* the definition of normality instead of reciting it;
1.7 because it forces the order of an element and Lagrange to be put together;
1.5 because it kills the assumption that normality passes along a chain.
Deliberately left: 1.1 and 1.6 (routine), 1.2 (we redo it properly in Chapter 3),
1.4 (a near-twin of 1.3), 1.8–1.10 (variations on 1.7 and the sign map).

We are on 1.3. You have already been given everything it needs; here it is in one place,
as facts, not as a lesson:

- $H \circ g = \{\, h \circ g : h \in H \,\}$, and $|H \circ g| = |H|$.
- The right cosets of $H$ **partition** $G$ — distinct cosets are disjoint, and every
  element of $G$ lies in one.
- $H \circ g = H$ if and only if $g \in H$.
- Garling's definition: $H$ is **normal** in $G$ when $g^{-1} \circ h \circ g \in H$
  for every $g \in G$ and every $h \in H$.
- The **index** of $H$ in $G$ is the number of distinct right cosets of $H$ in $G$.

---

> **1.3** Suppose that $H$ is a subgroup of $G$ of index 2. Show that $H$ is a
> normal subgroup of $G$.

Where to start: index 2 says there are exactly **two** right cosets, and one of them is
$H$ itself. Write down what the other one has to be, in terms of $H$ and $G$, before
you do anything else.

Two remarks so you do not waste effort. $G$ is **not** assumed finite — nothing here
needs counting. And you must end at Garling's conjugation condition above: if your
argument runs through $H \circ g = g \circ H$ instead, you owe a line saying why that
gives the conjugation condition.

Write it on the slate and send.
