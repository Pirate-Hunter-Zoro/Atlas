Two, and your answer is right — with one word changed. "How many you need" should read "the FEWEST you need".

That fewest is the degree [L:k]. Your {1, i} spans ℂ over ℝ, since every a+bi is a·1 + b·i with a and b real, and no single vector does it, so [ℂ:ℝ] = 2. The word "fewest" is load-bearing. The set {1, i, 1+i} also spans ℂ, but 1+i is already reachable from the other two, so it is redundant and that set is not a basis. Every basis of the same space has the same size, and that common size is the degree.

One tiny piece first, and one symbol answers it. Dimension 1 means a single vector spans M over k. Name an element of L that you already know lies in M and can serve as that single basis vector.

what the question uses:
- k ⊆ M ⊆ L: M is an intermediate field — a subfield of L containing k.
- [L:k]: the dimension of L as a vector space over k, which is the size of a basis.
- tower law: [L:k] = [L:M][M:k], for any intermediate M.
- basis: a spanning set with nothing redundant in it.
- spans: every element is a sum of basis vectors, each times one coefficient from k.
- dimension 1: a basis with exactly one element.
- M is a field, so 1 ∈ M; and 1 ∈ k as well.
