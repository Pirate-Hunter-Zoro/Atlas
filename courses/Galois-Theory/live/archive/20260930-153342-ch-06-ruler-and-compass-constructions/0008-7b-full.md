Right: $\sigma(y^{-1})=\sigma(y)^{-1}$, and your chain is the proof. No question on the page, so this is grading only.

Labelled, it reads:

$$\sigma(y^{-1})\,\sigma(y)=\sigma(y^{-1}y)\ \text{(multiplies)}=\sigma(1)\ \text{(inverse)}=1\ \text{(fixes }F\text{, as }1\in F).$$

One gap. Dividing by $\sigma(y)$ needs $\sigma(y)\neq0$. That holds because $\sigma$ is injective and $\sigma(0)=0$. Your chain actually shows it for free: $\sigma(y^{-1})\sigma(y)=1$ cannot happen if $\sigma(y)=0$.

One slip in your statement at the top. $\sigma$ is determined by the *images* $\sigma(\alpha_1),\dots,\sigma(\alpha_n)$, not by $\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n$ themselves.

You now have every tool 7(b) needs.

**Problem 7(b).** Let $K/F$ be an extension, $K=F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$, and $\sigma,\tau\in\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)$. If $\sigma(\alpha_i)=\tau(\alpha_i)$ for every $i$, then $\sigma=\tau$.

What it uses:

- $\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)$: isomorphisms $K\to K$ fixing every $c\in F$.
- Adds: $\sigma(x+y)=\sigma(x)+\sigma(y)$.
- Multiplies: $\sigma(xy)=\sigma(x)\sigma(y)$.
- Inverses: $\sigma(y^{-1})=\sigma(y)^{-1}$ for $y\neq0$, just proved.
- $F(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$: every element is $p(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)/q(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)$, with $p,q$ polynomials over $F$ and $q(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_n)\neq0$.

Your move: prove that if $\sigma(\alpha_i)=\tau(\alpha_i)$ for every $i$, then $\sigma(x)=\tau(x)$ for every $x\in K$. Take $x=p(\alpha)/q(\alpha)$ and push both maps through it, labelling each step.
