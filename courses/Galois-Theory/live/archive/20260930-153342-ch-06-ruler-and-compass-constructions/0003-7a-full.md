Yes. An automorphism of $K$ over $F$ is exactly that: a field isomorphism $\sigma:K\to K$ with $\sigma(c)=c$ for every $c\in F$. Moving on to the proof.

**Problem 7(a).** Let $K/F$ be an extension and let $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)$. Show that if $f\in F[x]$ and $f(\alpha)=0$ for some $\alpha\in K$, then $f(\sigma(\alpha))=0$.

What the problem uses:

- $\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)$: the isomorphisms $\sigma:K\to K$ with $\sigma(c)=c$ for all $c\in F$.
- Write $f(x)=\sum_{j=0}^{n} c_j x^j$ with every $c_j\in F$.
- "Adds": $\sigma(a+b)=\sigma(a)+\sigma(b)$.
- "Multiplies": $\sigma(ab)=\sigma(a)\sigma(b)$, so $\sigma(\alpha^j)=\sigma(\alpha)^j$.
- $\sigma(0)=0$, since $\sigma(0)=\sigma(0+0)=\sigma(0)+\sigma(0)$.

Your move: prove that if $f=\sum_j c_j x^j\in F[x]$ and $f(\alpha)=0$ with $\alpha\in K$, then $f(\sigma(\alpha))=0$ for every $\sigma\in\operatorname{Aut}(K/F)$.
