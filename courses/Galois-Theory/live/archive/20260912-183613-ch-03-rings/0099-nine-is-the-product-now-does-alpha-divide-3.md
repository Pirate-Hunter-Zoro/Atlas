$9$, and $2 - i\sqrt{5}$ is in $R$. So
$$\alpha \cdot (2 - i\sqrt{5}) = 9$$
is a genuine divisibility in $R$: $\alpha \mid 9$. That is your first check, and
it cost one multiplication.

And $9 = 3 \cdot 3$ — the splitting you wrote down yourself two revisions ago,
when you were listing the factorizations of $9$ in $\mathbb{N}$.

So the pair is sitting there. What is left is the other two checks, and by
symmetry they are one check done once.
