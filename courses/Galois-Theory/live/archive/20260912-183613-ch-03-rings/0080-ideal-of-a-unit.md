Correct. $(2) = \mathbb{Q}$, the whole ring, and the $r$ that does it is
$r = q/2$ — which exists precisely because $2$ has an inverse there.

One instance is not a theorem, so here is the one that disagrees with it.

In $\mathbb{Z}$, the same definition:
$$(2) = \{2r : r \in \mathbb{Z}\}.$$

Here $2$ has no inverse. And this time $(2)$ really is the even ones — it is a
proper subset of $\mathbb{Z}$, not all of it.

So the two ideals are built by the identical recipe from the identical element,
and one is everything while the other is not.
