Right on both counts: $1$ and $-1$ are units, each its own inverse.

The rest of the question has an answer that never looks at another element of
$R$. $R$ is infinite. You are not going to rule its elements out one at a time,
and nothing in 3.12 asks you to.

Instead: being a unit is *one equation*. Push that equation through $\varphi$
and it stops being a statement about elements of $R$ and becomes a statement
about non-negative integers — where there is almost no room to move.

That is what $\varphi$ is for, and it is why the problem hands it to you before
(b) and (c) as well.

---

**Your move.** Suppose $\alpha \in R$ is a unit, so $\alpha\beta = 1$ for some
$\beta \in R$.

Apply $\varphi$ to both sides. Write down the equation you get, and say which
set the two quantities in it live in.
