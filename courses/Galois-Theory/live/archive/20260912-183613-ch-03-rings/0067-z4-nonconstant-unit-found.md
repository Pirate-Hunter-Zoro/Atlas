Correct. $4x^{2} + 12x + 9$, and mod $4$ that is $0 + 0 + 1$. So $2x+3$ is a
unit of $\mathbb{Z}_{4}[x]$ of degree $1$, its own inverse — impossible in
$R[x]$ for $R$ an integral domain, and it exists here for exactly the reason
you gave: $2 \cdot 2 = 0$ kills the top coefficient, and $4 \mid 12$ kills the
middle one. Typesetting it now.
