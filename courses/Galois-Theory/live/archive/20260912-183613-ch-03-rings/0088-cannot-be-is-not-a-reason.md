title: The reduction to $\{R\}$ is right. "Cannot be" is not a reason.
---

Everything down to the last clause is right, and it is the right shape: you took
the contrapositive, said so, and reduced.

$a$ a unit gives $(a) = R$, hence
$$R/(a) = R/R = \{R\},$$
one element — the single coset $R$ itself. That is the substance, and it is
correct.

Then:

> which cannot be an integral domain

That is the thing you were asked to prove, restated in the position where its
proof should be. Compare the last two pages: the same move, one level up.

The definition you were handed has **two** requirements. In the one-element ring
$\{R\}$ one of them holds perfectly well and the other fails. Naming which, and
saying why, *is* the proof — it is one line, and it is the only line still owed.

- **Integral domain:** a commutative ring in which $e \neq 0$, and in which
  $xy = 0$ forces $x = 0$ or $y = 0$.
