**3.17.** Let $p$ be a prime number, and let

$$R = \left\{\, x \in \mathbb{Q} : x = r/s \text{ for some } r,s \in \mathbb{Z} \text{ with } p \nmid s \,\right\}.$$

Show that $R$ is a subring of $\mathbb{Q}$. Determine the units of $R$. Show
that $R$ is a principal ideal domain.

(Garling's sheet prints "show that $S$ is a subring" — a typo. There is no
$S$; it means $R$.)

Available:

- *Subring:* a subset containing $0$ and $1$, closed under addition,
  negation and multiplication. It is then a ring under the inherited
  operations.
- *Unit:* $u \in R$ is a unit if $uv = 1$ for some $v \in R$ — the inverse
  must itself lie in $R$.
- *Ideal:* $I \subseteq R$ closed under addition, and $ra \in I$ whenever
  $r \in R$, $a \in I$.
- *Principal ideal:* $(a) = \{ra : r \in R\}$, the ideal generated by one
  element.
- *Integral domain:* a commutative ring with $1 \neq 0$ and no zero
  divisors.
- *Principal ideal domain:* an integral domain in which every ideal is
  principal.
- *Divides:* $c \mid a$ in $R$ means $a = rc$ for some $r \in R$;
  equivalently $a \in (c)$ (yours, 3.14).
- *$p \nmid s$:* $p$ does not divide $s$ in $\mathbb{Z}$.
