Between the last page and this one exactly one thing moved: $R$ became $(a)$.
Nothing arrived to justify it. That line is now the statement to be proved,
written down as though it had been.

The break is one word above it, and it is the same word as last time:

> by the definition of $(a)$, $\forall r \in R$

The definition is
$$(a) = \{ra : r \in R\}.$$

It does not hand you every $r$. Saying $x \in (a)$ says there **exists** one
$r \in R$ with $x = ra$. Membership is an $\exists$, not a $\forall$ — and that
is why your $r$ never got used anywhere on either page. A $\forall r$ gives you
nothing to pick up and carry; an $\exists r$ hands you a specific element of
$R$, and that element is the whole argument.

So you have $e \in (a)$, and you got it honestly. It therefore comes with a
witness.
