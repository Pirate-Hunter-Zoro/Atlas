The integer part is right: $ab = 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ has exactly the two
solutions $(1,1)$ and $(-1,-1)$. That settles card 0061 — $-1$ is a unit of
$\mathbb{Z}[x]$ and is its own inverse, so *only the identity* is dead.

The break is the line after it:

> so units in $R[x]$ are $R(x) = 1_{R}$ and $R(x) = -1_{R}$ — the $1$-element
> and its additive inverse.

That is $\mathbb{Z}$'s answer, promoted to every $R$. I gave you $\mathbb{Z}$
because it was the smallest place to find a second unit, not because it is
typical. $\mathbb{Z}$ happens to have exactly two elements with multiplicative
inverses; another integral domain need not.

You have the right *shape* — a unit of $R[x]$ is a constant, and that constant
has to be invertible. What you have not got is which constants those are, and
you cannot read it off $\mathbb{Z}$.
