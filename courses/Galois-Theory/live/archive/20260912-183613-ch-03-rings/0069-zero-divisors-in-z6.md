Correct: $0+(3)$, $3+(3)$, $6+(3)$, and the pattern is the one 3.11 runs on —
$b + (a)$ is the zero of $R/(a)$ exactly when $a \mid b$.

Now the other half of the machinery. $3$ is prime and $\mathbb{Z}/(3)$ behaved.
Take a non-prime instead and watch what goes wrong.

---

**Your move.** Take $R = \mathbb{Z}$ and $a = 6$, so we are in
$\mathbb{Z}/(6)$, whose elements are $0+(6), 1+(6), \ldots, 5+(6)$.

Find two classes $b + (6)$ and $c + (6)$, **neither of them the zero class**,
whose product is the zero class:
$$(b + (6))(c + (6)) = 0 + (6).$$

Give $b$ and $c$, and the product $bc$ that shows it.

Recall the multiplication: $(b+(6))(c+(6)) = bc + (6)$, and by what you just
did, $bc + (6)$ is zero exactly when $6 \mid bc$.
