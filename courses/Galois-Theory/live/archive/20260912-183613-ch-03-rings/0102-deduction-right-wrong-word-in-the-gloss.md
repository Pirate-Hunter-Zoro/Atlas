Irreducible, not prime, therefore not a unique factorization domain. That is the
deduction and it is correct. I am transcribing it now.

Then you wrote:

> an element in $R$ could have multiple different **prime** factorizations

Not *prime*. Factorization into **primes** is essentially unique wherever it
exists — that is a theorem, and it holds in any integral domain, this one
included. Nothing about $R$ breaks it.

What fails here is uniqueness of factorization into **irreducibles**. That is
what the "U" in UFD is about, and it is why the exercise made you prove
irreducibility in (b) and non-primality in (c) as two separate things.

The instance is already on your page:
$$9 = 3 \cdot 3 = (2 + i\sqrt{5})(2 - i\sqrt{5}).$$
