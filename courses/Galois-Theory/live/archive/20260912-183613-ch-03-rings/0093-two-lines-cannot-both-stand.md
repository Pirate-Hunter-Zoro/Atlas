and $\varphi(\beta) = \varphi(\alpha)$ is a fair reading of it.

And you wrote the key observation yourself, unprompted:

$$\varphi : R \to \mathbb{N}.$$

That is the line the entire argument turns on — it is what makes an infinite
ring finite to search. Then the line directly beneath it says

$$\varphi(\alpha) \in \{-1, 1\}.$$

Both cannot stand. If $\varphi$ lands in $\mathbb{N}$, then $-1$ was never a
candidate and does not need ruling out.

$\varphi$ is:
$$\varphi(m + i\sqrt{5}\,n) = m^{2} + 5n^{2},$$
a sum of two quantities, neither of which is ever negative, for any integers
$m$ and $n$.
