Everything the statement uses:

- *Integral domain:* a commutative ring with $1 \neq 0$ in which $rs = 0$ implies $r = 0$ or $s = 0$.
- *Field of fractions of an integral domain $D$:* the field of formal quotients $a/b$ with $a, b \in D$ and $b \neq 0$, where $a/b = c/d$ exactly when $ad = bc$. $D$ sits inside it by $a \mapsto a/1$.
- *$R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$:* polynomials in $n$ indeterminates with coefficients in $R$ — itself an integral domain.
- *$F(x_1,\ldots,x_n)$:* by definition the field of fractions of $F[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$.
- *Identified naturally:* exhibit an isomorphism between the two fields that fixes $R$ and each $x_i$.
