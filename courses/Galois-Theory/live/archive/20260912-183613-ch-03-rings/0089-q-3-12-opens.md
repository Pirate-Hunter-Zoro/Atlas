## 3.12

Let $R = \mathbb{Z} + i\sqrt{5}\,\mathbb{Z} = \{m + i\sqrt{5}\,n : m,n \in
\mathbb{Z}\}$, a subring of $\mathbb{C}$.

**(a)** Show that the units of $R$ are $1$ and $-1$.

**(b)** Let $\varphi(m + i\sqrt{5}\,n) = m^{2} + 5n^{2}$, so $\varphi(0) = 0$,
$\varphi(1) = \varphi(-1) = 1$, and otherwise $\varphi(\alpha) > 3$. Use this to
show that $2 + i\sqrt{5}$ and $2 - i\sqrt{5}$ are irreducible in $R$.

**(c)** Show that $2 + i\sqrt{5}$ is not prime, and hence that $R$ is not a
unique factorization domain.

**What the statement uses.**

- *Unit:* $\alpha \in R$ such that $\alpha\beta = 1$ for some $\beta \in R$.
- *Irreducible:* $\alpha$ non-zero, not a unit, and $\alpha = \beta\gamma$
  forces $\beta$ or $\gamma$ to be a unit.
- *Prime:* $\alpha$ non-zero, not a unit, and $\alpha \mid \beta\gamma$ forces
  $\alpha \mid \beta$ or $\alpha \mid \gamma$.
- *UFD:* every non-zero non-unit is a product of irreducibles, unique up to
  order and unit factors.
