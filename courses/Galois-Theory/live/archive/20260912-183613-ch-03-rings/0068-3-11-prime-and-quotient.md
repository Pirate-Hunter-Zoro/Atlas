**3.11 (Garling).** Show that an element $a$ of an integral domain $R$ is prime
if and only if $R/(a)$ is an integral domain.

Not yet — one rung first.

- *Prime element:* $a \neq 0$, $a$ not a unit, and whenever $a \mid bc$ then
  $a \mid b$ or $a \mid c$.
- $(a) = \{ra : r \in R\}$, the ideal generated by $a$.
- $R/(a)$: the classes $b + (a)$, added and multiplied by
  $(b + (a))(c + (a)) = bc + (a)$. Its zero is $0 + (a) = (a)$.
- *Integral domain:* commutative, $1 \neq 0$, no zero divisors.
- $a \mid b$ means $b = ra$ for some $r \in R$.
