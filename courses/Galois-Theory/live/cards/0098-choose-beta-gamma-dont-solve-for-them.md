Your unpacking of *not prime* is right, and the second version of it is right in
the harder way: $\exists c_{1}$ with $\alpha c_{1} = \beta\gamma$, but no
$c_{2}$ with $\alpha c_{2} = \beta$ or $\alpha c_{2} = \gamma$. That is the
definition in correct logical shape, written unprompted.

Then read your own line back. The $\exists$ in it is **yours to satisfy**.

*Not prime* is an existence claim. You are not proving something about every
$\beta$ and $\gamma$ — you are producing **one specific pair** and checking three
things about it:

1. $\alpha$ divides $\beta\gamma$;
2. $\alpha$ does not divide $\beta$;
3. $\alpha$ does not divide $\gamma$.

Three checks, on numbers you pick yourself.

That is why $9\,\varphi(c) = \varphi(\beta)\varphi(\gamma)$ went nowhere. In
that equation $\beta$, $\gamma$ and $c$ are all still unknown, so there is
nothing to solve for — you have written one equation about three things nobody
has chosen yet. The choosing is the work.

Your last line — *"part of $\beta$" and "part of $\gamma$" can't form $\alpha$
because $\alpha$ is irreducible* — is true, and it will not be needed. (b) is
already banked; (c) does not lean on it.
