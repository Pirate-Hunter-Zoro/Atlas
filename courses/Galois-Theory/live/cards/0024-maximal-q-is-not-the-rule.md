$d = 6$ is correct. $6a = 3x^{2} + 4x$ and $6b = 5x + 18$, both in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$. That part is settled.

The rule you attached to it is not. The denominators occurring in $a$ and $b$ are $2$, $3$ and $6$, and the largest of them is $6$ — which happens to be the same number that works. On this example the right answer and your rule coincide, and that is the example's doing, not the rule's.

So test it on a pair where they come apart.

**Your move.** Take

$$a = \tfrac{1}{2}x + \tfrac{1}{3}, \qquad b = 1 .$$

Your rule says: take the largest $q$ among the coefficients $p/q$. Name the $d$ that gives, write out $da$, and say whether $da \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$.
