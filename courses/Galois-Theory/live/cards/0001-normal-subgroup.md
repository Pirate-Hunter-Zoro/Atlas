title: Normal subgroup
---
A subgroup $H \le G$ is **normal** (written $H \normal G$) if conjugation by any element of $G$ sends $H$ to itself.

Two equivalent ways to say it:

1. For every $g \in G$, $gHg^{-1} = H$.
2. For every $g \in G$, $gHg^{-1} \subseteq H$ (the reverse inclusion is automatic once this holds for all $g$).

A useful pointwise reformulation: $H$ is normal iff
$$g h g^{-1} \in H \quad \text{for all } g \in G,\ h \in H.$$
In particular, when $H$ is abelian, conjugation collapses to the identity and $H$ is automatically normal — so every subgroup of an abelian group is normal.

A few quick landmarks to anchor the definition:

- Every subgroup of index $2$ is normal. (You did this last sitting: the two cosets are $H$ and its complement, and any $g \notin H$ must swap them, sending $H$ to itself.)
- The trivial subgroup $\{e\}$ and $G$ itself are always normal.
- In $S_3$, the three subgroups of order $2$ — generated by $(1\,2)$, $(1\,3)$, $(2\,3)$ — are **not** normal: conjugating $(1\,2)$ by $(1\,2\,3)$ gives $(2\,3) \notin \gen{(1\,2)}$. The unique subgroup of order $3$, $A_3$, *is* normal.

A first test to make the definition mechanical, on the board now: in $S_3$ with $h = (1\,2)$ and $g = (1\,2\,3)$, compute $g h g^{-1}$ and report its order.
