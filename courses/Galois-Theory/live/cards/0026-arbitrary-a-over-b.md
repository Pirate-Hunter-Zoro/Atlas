Two: the tuple is $(0, \tfrac23, \tfrac12, 0, 0, \ldots)$. And that is not special to this polynomial. Every polynomial has finitely many non-zero coefficients and *all but finitely many* entries equal to $0_R$ — which is the reverse of what your margin note says, and you have just shown it yourself. Fix the note; it is the hypothesis your $d$ depends on.

**Your move.** Let $a/b \in \mathbb{Q}(x)$ be arbitrary, with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$ and $b \neq 0$. Produce an integer $d$ such that $da$ and $db$ both lie in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$, say why such a $d$ exists for *every* $a$ and $b$, and write down the resulting pair in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$ representing the same element of $\mathbb{Q}(x)$.

What the statement uses:

- $\mathbb{Q}(x) = \operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Q}[x])$: its elements are classes of pairs $(a,b)$ with $a, b \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, $b \neq 0$, written $a/b$.
- $a/b = c/e$ exactly when $ae = bc$.
- Hence $a/b = (da)/(db)$ for any $d \neq 0$: same class, different representative.
- A polynomial in $\mathbb{Q}[x]$ has finitely many non-zero coefficients, each of the form $p/q$ with $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}$, $q \neq 0$.
