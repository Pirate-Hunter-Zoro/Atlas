$x \neq 0$ is in the proof now, and the conclusion says *nonzero*. Both correct.
And the step you circled is the right step to have circled — that expansion,
$x^{n-1}x = x \implies x^{n-1} = e$, is where the hypothesis earns its keep.

What is left is the word **meaning**, in line two.

*The powers repeat* gives you two equal powers. It does not, by itself, give you
that one of them is $x$.

Here is the ring that shows the gap. In $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$, take $x = 2$:
$$2, \quad 2^{2} = 0, \quad 2^{3} = 0, \quad 2^{4} = 0, \ \ldots$$
Those repeat. Is there any $n \geq 2$ with $2^{n} = 2$? No. Your line two, read
as written, would say there is.

$\mathbb{Z}_{4}$ is finite and has $e \neq 0$. What it is not is an integral
domain. So your line two is true in $R$ — but it is *using* something, and it
does not say what.
