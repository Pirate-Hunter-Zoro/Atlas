**3.10 (Garling).** What are the units in $R[x]$, where $R$ is an integral
domain? What are the units in $\mathbb{Z}_{4}[x]$?

**Your move.** Answer both, with reasons. The first is the coefficient you just
computed, turned on $fg = 1$; the second is the $\mathbb{Z}_{6}$ collapse you
saw in the rung before it, put to work.

---

What the statement uses:

- *Unit:* $u$ in a ring $S$ is a unit if there is $v \in S$ with $uv = 1_{S}$.
  In $R[x]$ the identity $1_{S}$ is the constant polynomial $1$.
- *Integral domain:* commutative, $1 \neq 0$, no zero divisors — $ab = 0$
  forces $a = 0$ or $b = 0$.
- $R[x]$: polynomials $\sum_{i}a_{i}x^{i}$ with $a_{i} \in R$, finitely many
  non-zero. $R \subseteq R[x]$ as the constants.
- $\deg f$: the largest $i$ with $a_{i} \neq 0$; undefined for $f = 0$. A
  non-zero constant has degree $0$.
- $\mathbb{Z}_{4}$: the integers mod $4$. Not an integral domain: $2 \cdot 2 = 0$.
- *Yours, from the rungs:* for non-zero $f, g \in R[x]$ over an integral domain
  $R$, the coefficient of $x^{\deg f + \deg g}$ in $fg$ is $a_{n}b_{m} \neq 0$,
  so $\deg(fg) = \deg f + \deg g$. Over $\mathbb{Z}_{6}$ that failed:
  $(2x+1)(3x+1) = 5x+1$.

Note the second question wants an actual answer, not just "the argument breaks".
