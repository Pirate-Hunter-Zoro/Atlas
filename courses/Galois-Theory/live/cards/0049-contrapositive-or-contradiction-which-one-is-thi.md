title: Contrapositive or contradiction — which one is this?
---
The arrows you drew point the right way — but look at what is sitting on top of each.

"Suppose not finite", "Suppose not cyclic", "Suppose not prime order" — these are three *negations of the conclusion*, stacked. That is the shape of a **contradiction** proof: you assume the conclusion is false and watch the hypothesis break.

The other direction (the $\Leftarrow$ arrow in the biconditional) will want the contrapositive: assume $G$ is *not* generated by some non-identity element, and read off what $G$ must be.

Two questions, one for each arrow:

- On the $\Rightarrow$ arrow, do you have one ingredient you have not yet used — that every non-identity element generates $G$? What does that give you about $|G|$?
- On the $\Leftarrow$ arrow, what does "not generated by some non-identity element" actually mean — in one symbol?

Start wherever you like; but write one line of *real* working, not a heading.
