Fair enough. You have circled this five times and that is my fault, not yours — take it stated.

The domain is **the field of fractions of $R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$**. Its elements are classes $p/q$ with $p, q \in R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]$ and $q \neq 0$. So:

$$\phi : \operatorname{Frac}\bigl(R[x_1,\ldots,x_n]\bigr) \longrightarrow F(x_1,\ldots,x_n).$$

That is all 3.2's sentence was ever saying. Both ends are now fields, and both ends now hold classes of pairs of polynomials — the difference is only which ring the polynomials come from: $R$ on the left, $F$ on the right.

Drop it to the smallest case, $R = \mathbb{Z}$, $n = 1$:

| | elements are classes $p/q$ where |
|---|---|
| $\operatorname{Frac}(\mathbb{Z}[x])$ | $p, q \in \mathbb{Z}[x]$, $q \neq 0$ |
| $\mathbb{Q}(x)$ | $p, q \in \mathbb{Q}[x]$, $q \neq 0$ |

Integer coefficients on the left, rational coefficients on the right, and 3.2 claims these two fields are the same. $\dfrac{x^2+1}{3x}$ is plainly in both.

The one that is not obvious is the other direction — something written with rational coefficients, landing in the left column.

**Your move.** $\dfrac{\tfrac{1}{2}x + 1}{x}$ is an element of $\mathbb{Q}(x)$. Write it as $p/q$ with $p$ and $q$ both in $\mathbb{Z}[x]$.
