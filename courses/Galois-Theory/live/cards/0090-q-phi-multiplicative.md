Correct, all four: $\alpha\beta = 7 + i\sqrt{5}$, and $\varphi$ takes the values
$6$, $9$, $54$. And you noticed $\varphi(\alpha\beta) =
\varphi(\alpha)\varphi(\beta)$ without being told to look for it, which is the
whole reason that computation was set.

But one instance is not a theorem, and you have been caught by exactly that
before — $\pm 1$ in $\mathbb{Z}$ became the units of every $R[x]$. All three
parts of 3.12 run on this identity holding for **every** $\alpha$ and $\beta$
in $R$, so it has to be earned.

A handle, should you want it: $R$ sits inside $\mathbb{C}$, and for
$\alpha = m + i\sqrt{5}\,n$ the complex conjugate
$$\bar{\alpha} = m - i\sqrt{5}\,n$$
is again an element of $R$. You are not obliged to use it — expanding both
sides directly also works.
