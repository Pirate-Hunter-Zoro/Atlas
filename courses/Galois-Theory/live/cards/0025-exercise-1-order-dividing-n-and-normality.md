**Exercise 1.5 (Garling).** Suppose that $H$ is a normal subgroup of $G$ and that $K$ is a normal subgroup of $H$. Is $K$ necessarily a normal subgroup of $G$?

That is the whole statement — it is asked as a question, so your answer starts with **yes** or **no**, and the two answers cost very different things.

If **yes**: you owe a proof. Take $k \in K$ and $g \in G$ and show $gkg^{-1} \in K$, using only that $gHg^{-1} = H$ and $hKh^{-1} = K$ for $h \in H$.

If **no**: you owe one explicit counterexample — a named group $G$, a named subgroup $H$ normal in it, a named subgroup $K$ normal in $H$, and one specific $g \in G$ and $k \in K$ with $gkg^{-1} \notin K$. Nothing less disproves it, and nothing more is needed.

Try the proof first. Write out what you are given and what you want, and see whether the conjugation you are handed is the conjugation you need. Where it jams is the answer.

**Your move:** answer the exercise.
