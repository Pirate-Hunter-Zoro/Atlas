Right. $y^2 = (gxg^{-1})(gxg^{-1}) = gx^2g^{-1}$, the inner $g^{-1}g$ dying in the middle, and the same collapse repeated $k$ times gives

$$y^k = gx^kg^{-1}.$$

That identity is the engine of the next exercise, and you now own it.

Your last line — *so $y$ has the same order as $x$* — is the right destination, and you put an ellipsis after it, which I read as you knowing there is a step there you have not said out loud. There is. It is one line, and it is the line the exercise actually runs on.

Order is defined by which powers give the identity: $x$ has order $n$ when $x^n = e$ and no smaller positive power does. So to compare the order of $y$ with the order of $x$, you have to compare *which $k$ make each of them trivial*. Your identity turns one into the other, but only after you get from $gx^kg^{-1}$ back to $x^k$.

**Your move:** suppose $gx^kg^{-1} = e$ for some particular $k$. In one line, show that $x^k = e$.

Nothing clever is needed — it is the cancellation you have already done twice. Multiply both sides by the right thing on the right side, and by the right thing on the left.
