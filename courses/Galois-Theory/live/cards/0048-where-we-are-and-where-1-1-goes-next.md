§1.1 is essentially done. Here is the shape of what you have settled:

- **Cycle arithmetic.** Right factor first; conjugation by $\sigma$ relabels a cycle $\sigma \tau \sigma^{-1}$ by applying $\sigma$ to each entry.
- **Cycle type survives conjugation**, and the order of a cycle equals its length.
- **Generating sets.** $\gen{(1\,2),\,(1\,2\,3)} = \Sym_3$ — six elements, written out and checked.
- **Normality.** Index $2$ forces normality (Ex 1.3, one-line proof); normality also passes through quotients (Ex 1.5).
- **The cycle-type test for normality.** A subgroup of $\Sym_n$ is normal iff it is a union of conjugacy classes. You ran it on $\Sym_3$ (sees three transpositions → fails) and on the Klein four-group $K \le \Sym_4$ (transpositions go elsewhere → fails).
- **Cyclic subgroups.** For $g$ in a finite group, $\gen{g}$ is the loop $e, g, g^2, \dots$ until it returns to $e$; the order is the length of that loop.
