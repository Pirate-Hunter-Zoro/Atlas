You dropped "$0$ is not mapped", which was the right move. The replacement is still false, and it fails for a reason worth having.

You wrote: *nothing maps to any $a/b$ with $b \neq 1_R$.*

Take $4/2$. Its denominator is $2 \neq 1_R$, so your claim says no integer maps to it. But $2 \mapsto 2/1$, and $2/1 = 4/2$ — check it against the relation you wrote one line up: $2 \cdot 2 = 4 \cdot 1$. So $4/2$ **is** in the image, and it is one of your own excluded ones.

The break is not arithmetic. It is that "$b \neq 1_R$" is a property of a **representative**, not of the element. Elements of $F$ are equivalence classes; a single class has infinitely many names, $2/1$ and $4/2$ and $-6/{-3}$, and they differ in what $b$ is. So no condition written on $b$ alone can pick out a set of elements — you have described a property of *how something is written*, and the thing you want to describe is what it *is*.

Which is why the question asked for **one element**, not a rule. A single concrete rational, where you can say plainly that *no* integer maps to it — that is a claim about every way of writing it, and it will force you to say the right thing.

**Your move.** Name one specific element of $\mathbb{Q}$ that no integer maps to, and give the one-line reason.
