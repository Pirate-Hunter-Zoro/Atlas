Your chain is right, and the three lines you have under the ellipsis are exactly the line I asked for.

Starting from $gx^kg^{-1} = e$:

- right-multiply by $g$: $\ gx^k = g$, hence $gx^k g^{-1} = e \Leftrightarrow gx^k = g$ — but you took the cleaner route, left-multiplying by $g^{-1}$ to get $x^kg^{-1} = g^{-1}$, then right-multiplying by $g$ to get $x^k = e$.

The $g$ cancels because $g$ is invertible — that is the whole content. And the reverse direction is the same three lines run backwards, so you actually have an "if and only if":

$$gx^kg^{-1} = e \ \Longleftrightarrow\ x^k = e.$$

That is the punchline of Exercise 1.4: the *same* $k$ makes $x^k$ and $y^k$ trivial, so $\ord(y) = \ord(x)$.

The handwriting is ambiguous; in the typeset version write $gxg^{-1}$ and the chain $y^k = gx^kg^{-1}$ reads cleanly.
