My fault. I asked for $g(4)$ without ever saying what that bracket means. Back up one step.

A permutation is not only a thing you multiply. It is a **function** on the points $1,2,3,4$: feed it a number, it hands you back a number. The cycle notation *is* the instruction sheet for that function.

Take $g = (2\ 3)$. Read the cycle left to right, wrapping at the end:

- $g(2) = 3$ — the cycle says $2$ goes to $3$
- $g(3) = 2$ — end of the cycle, wrap back to the front
- $g(1) = 1$ — $1$ is not written in the cycle, so it does not move
- $g(4) = 4$ — same reason

That is all $g(4)$ ever meant: *where does $g$ send $4$*. You already do this — it is the "track a point" move you used to multiply permutations, just with a name and a bracket on it.

Nothing about card 0030 needs to be understood yet. It only becomes a rule once this bracket is comfortable, so we are stopping here.

**Your move.** Let $\sigma = (1\ 3\ 4)$, a permutation of $\{1,2,3,4\}$. Write down all four values:

$$\sigma(1) = ?,\qquad \sigma(2) = ?,\qquad \sigma(3) = ?,\qquad \sigma(4) = ?$$

Four numbers. No multiplying, no inverses.
