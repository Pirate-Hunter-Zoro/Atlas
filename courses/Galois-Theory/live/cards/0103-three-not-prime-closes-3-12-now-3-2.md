## 3.2

Suppose that $R$ is an integral domain, with field of fractions $F$. Show that
the field of fractions of $R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$ can be identified naturally
with $F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})$.

What the statement uses:

- *Integral domain:* commutative, $e \neq 0$, and no zero divisors.
- *Field of fractions $F$ of $R$:* classes of pairs $(a,b)$ with $b \neq 0$,
  written $a/b$, where $a/b = c/d$ means $ad = bc$. $R$ sits inside it by
  $a \mapsto a/e$.
- *$R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$:* polynomials in $n$ indeterminates over $R$; it is
  itself an integral domain, so it has a field of fractions.
- *$F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})$:* the field of fractions of $F[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$.
- *Identified naturally with:* an isomorphism, and one that is the identity on
  $R[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]$.
