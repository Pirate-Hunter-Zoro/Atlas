The run is all eleven exercises of chapter 4. Order: 4.1, 4.4, 4.6, 4.5,
then 4.2, 4.7-4.11, with 4.3 last. 4.1 is closed. 4.4 is three-quarters done.

Where they got to. 4.1 is finished and correct: tower law, the complete
degree-7 pair list (7,1) and (1,7), both degree-1 cases, and the answer that
the only intermediate fields are K and L. On 4.4 they proved x^3 + 3x + 1
irreducible over Q unaided -- constant term 1, candidates 1 and -1, values 5
and -3, no rational root, and degree 3 splits only as 1 + 2. They then got
alpha^{-1} = -alpha^2 - 3 with the coefficients named as rational. None of
that gets re-taught.

What was wrong. One slip, self-corrected once named: they factored
alpha^3 + 3alpha as alpha(alpha + 3) instead of alpha(alpha^2 + 3), dropping
a power. The earlier real error was proof shape: on 4.1 they reached for a
contradiction where none was needed and fed the spanning property an
arbitrary element instead of 1. Shape, never algebra -- watch that.

Next thing to teach. Card 0009 is open. It asks only for the expansion of
(beta - 1)^3 + 3(beta - 1) + 1 with beta = 1 + alpha; expect
beta^3 - 3beta^2 + 6beta - 3. The point is the nonzero constant term, which
makes pull-out-and-divide work a second time. Then: pull beta out of the
first three terms, divide by 3, re-express in alpha, giving
(1 + alpha)^{-1} = (alpha^2 - alpha + 4)/3. That closes 4.4; then 4.6.

How this student works. Algebra and arithmetic are reliable; the gaps are
structural. They skip any check or warm-up they judge beneath the real
exercise, including ones asked for explicitly -- run those yourself inside
the next card instead of re-posing them. They copy the exercise out at the
top of the page, both halves at once, and that is not a question. They leave
the last justifying line unwritten when they think it obvious; supply it.
